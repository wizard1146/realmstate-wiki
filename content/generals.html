A general leads one army or commands your home defense. Each general has flat traits, such as Aggressive or Strategist, and applies them to the battles it's in.

Raising a general

  • Retire {{general_elites}} elites at home. Some races need a different number; see each race's page.
  • Pay {{general_cost}} {{general_material}} for each trait, times your general cost modifier.
  • A house keeps at most {{general_max}} generals.

Traits and renown

Your renown decides how many traits a new general can have. The renown needed for the first, second, third trait (and so on) is {{general_trait_renown}}.

Two things add a trait slot on top of what your renown gives: an Ancestral Hall, and the last tier of your state's Statesmanship project in the Colloquium.

Below {{general_pick_renown}} renown, the traits are drawn at random. From {{general_pick_renown}} renown, you choose them.

Example. With 250 renown you have 2 trait slots but can't choose yet: the general gets 2 random traits and costs twice {{general_cost}} {{general_material}}.

In battle

  • One general per army. It goes with the army and comes home with it.
  • Name one general your main general: it commands your defense while it's at home. Sending it on an attack ends that command until you name it again.
  • A general leading a failed attack is killed {{general_death_pct}}% of the time.

Service record

Every general keeps a record for as long as it lives: attacks led, victories and defeats, land taken, renown earned, defenses commanded and held as your main general, and every trade in the Hall of Deeds. You see your generals' records on your house page.
