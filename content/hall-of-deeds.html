The Hall of Deeds is where houses buy and sell generals and academics. Every character carries its service record, so a proven veteran is worth more than a fresh recruit.

Selling

Listing a character shows its name, traits or attributes and full record to everyone. You list it one of two ways:

  • Auction. Set a reserve price and a length of {{auction_min_ticks}} to {{auction_max_ticks}} ticks. Each bid must beat the last by {{bid_step_pct}}%. When the auction ends, the top bidder buys it. Once there's a bid, the auction runs to its end.
  • Open to offers. Anyone may offer gold; you accept or decline.

Buying

  • Bid on an auction, or make a direct offer on a character listed open to offers. You can't make an offer on a character its owner hasn't listed.
  • Your gold is held from the moment you bid or offer. It comes back at once if you're outbid, declined, withdraw, or the offer lapses after {{offer_ticks}} ticks.
  • You need room for it: fewer than {{general_max}} generals, or the universities for another academic.

What a sale costs

  • {{trade_fee_pct}}% of the price goes to the seller's state treasury.
  • A buyer from another realm pays {{out_of_realm_pct}}% on top. That extra goes to the character itself, recorded in its history as transfer income, and leaves the economy for good.
  • Every character's record keeps how often it has been traded, and its transfer fees: lifetime total, highest and average.

Example. A general sells for 10,000 gold to a house in another realm. The buyer pays {{hod_example_buyer_pays}}; the seller receives {{hod_example_seller_gets}} and their state {{hod_example_state_gets}}; the general's transfer income rises by {{hod_example_transfer}}.

What can't be traded

  • A general away with an army, or your main general.
  • A character that changed hands in the last {{trade_cooldown_ticks}} ticks.
  • Any character while either house's state is at war.

The war rule is checked again when a sale happens. If the buyer's state goes to war after a bid or offer, the auction ends without a sale or the offer can't be accepted, and the buyer's gold comes back.

A listed general can't lead an army or be named main general until you take it off the market.

Courted characters

A character that was courted and left wavering is auctioned here on its owner's behalf. The house that courted it has first refusal for the opening ticks; after that anyone may bid, and the owner is paid as in any sale.

Settling in

For {{settling_ticks}} ticks after a trade, a character is settling in with its new house: a general's army fights {{settling_penalty_size}}% weaker, and its house writes {{settling_penalty_size}}% fewer books for each settling academic.
