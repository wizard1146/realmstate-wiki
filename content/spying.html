Other houses' gold, food, peasants and troops are hidden (see What You Can See). Your thieves, the fourth unit slot, find them out, and can also steal and sabotage. Each thing they do is an operation; every operation this age is listed on Thieves' Operations.

Kinds of operation

  • Intel reads one part of a house: its estate, its muster roll, its ledgers, its archives or its recent news. A Full Dossier reads everything and costs the most.
  • Theft takes a share of their gold, food, a material, horses or books. What you get is capped by how many thieves you send.
  • Sabotage delays their construction, burns a building type or kills troops at home.

Nerve

Every house has Nerve, up to 100%. Each operation spends some (its cost is on the operations list). A failed operation costs {{nerve_fail_extra_pct}}% more on top. Nerve comes back at {{nerve_regen_pct}}% a tick. When you don't have enough, you can't send that operation.

Vigilance

Every attempt against a house, success or not, makes it more vigilant. Its Vigilance cuts the chance of the next operation against it by that much, up to {{vigilance_max_pct}}%, and fades by {{vigilance_decay_pct}}% a tick. A house can't be drained by a queue of thieves in one evening.

Chance of success

  1. Start from thieves per acre: yours sent, against theirs at home. Equal thieves per acre gives {{spy_base_success_pct}}%; formally {{spy_base_success_pct}}% × 2 × yours ÷ (yours + theirs), kept within 5%-95%. Thieves' dens make yours count for more; watch towers make theirs count for more.
  2. Multiply by the operation's own chance (intel is easier than sabotage).
  3. Take off their Vigilance.

The result is kept between 1% and 95%. Your operation's answer tells you the chance it had and the Nerve you have left.

If it works

  • Intel comes back as a report. Everyone in your state can read it; it stays in your state's reports. The target isn't told.
  • Theft is yours at once. Sabotage takes effect at once.
  • For theft and sabotage, the target's news says what happened, but not who did it.

If it fails

{{spy_loss_pct}}% of the thieves you sent are caught (at least one). The target's news says how many were caught, which operation it was, and which house sent them.

Limits

  • No operations on houses in your own state, or across a ceasefire.
  • Every attempt raises the target state's hostility meter toward yours: a little for intel ({{hostility_spy_points}} points), more for theft and most for sabotage.
  • At war, thefts, fires and killings take a bigger share.
  • Set Fires spends sulfur, win or lose.
