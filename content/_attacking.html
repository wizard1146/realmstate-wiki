An attack sends an army from your house against another. This page covers how the battle is fought, then every kind of attack and what limits how much a win takes. Building the army itself is on Military.

The battle

You choose how many troops of each unit slot to send, and the kind of attack: an Assault for land (the default), or Breach, Reclaim, Torch, Sack, Massacre or Blockade; within your own state, only Raze, for clearing out idle houses. Every kind fights the same battle; they differ in what a win takes and what they need. The battle resolves at once.

Armies out at once. Each attack sends an army, and it counts until it comes home. You may have {{armies_base}} armies out at once, and {{armies_per_general}} more for each general you have. With all out, an attack is refused and tells you when the first is back. A second strike joins the army already out.

Mercenaries. You can hire {{mercenary_name}} for an attack: {{mercenary_gold}} gold each (times your mercenary cost modifier), at most one for every {{mercenary_ratio}} of your own troops sent. They fight at {{mercenary_off}} offense, die like the rest, and the survivors leave after the battle instead of coming home. Some races differ: the Unlithians may hire more per troop sent, may hire them upgraded (upgraded_mercenaries in the attack, for the upgrade material), and keep some survivors as offensive specialists; see each race's page.

  1. Offense = the offense of every unit sent (elites times your elite offense modifier), times your offense modifier, times battle luck (up to ±{{luck_pct}}%). A general leading the army adds its traits.
  2. Defense = the defense of every unit the target has at home (elites times their elite defense modifier), times their defense modifier, plus 1 point per acre they own. Their defending general adds its traits.
  3. If offense is higher, you win. An assault takes {{land_gain_pct}}% of their land; other kinds take what the table below says. They lose it at once; you receive it when your army returns. You take more from a state that is Unfriendly or Hostile toward yours, and more still from one you're at war with.
  4. Win or lose, {{attacker_loss_pct}}% of the troops you sent die, less what your medics save. Every attack costs you at least {{attacker_min_losses}} troop(s), however small.
  5. Up to {{defender_loss_pct}}% of the target's troops at home die, less what their medics save. That full share dies when your offense at least matches their defense. A weaker attack kills in proportion: at half their defense, half as many die. A token army kills almost nobody.
  6. An attack with less than {{weak_attack_pct}}% of the target's defense is weak. It is fought as usual, but it counts for nothing else: it adds nothing to the hostility meter, doesn't count toward declaring war, can't put you among a mirroring race's attackers or afflict the target, and leaves no mark on their main general's record.
  7. An assault, breach or reclaim that wins earns renown.
  8. Sacked goods travel home with the army and arrive when it does.
  9. A house hit often lately loses less to each new hit, in land and in troops (see protection from repeated hits).
  10. Your army comes home {{attack_return_ticks}} ticks later, times your return time modifier.

Troops that are away don't defend your house.

You can't attack houses in your own state (except to raze an idle one), across a ceasefire, or while either house is under protection. No attack of any kind can be sent in the last {{attacks_close_ticks}} ticks of the age (see End of the Age). Every attack raises the target state's hostility meter toward yours.

What you learn

You see your own offense, whether you won, the land taken and the losses on both sides, but never the defender's defense. The defender's news tells them who attacked and what they lost. To size up a target first, spy on it.
