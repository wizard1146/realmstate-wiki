Connecting from another site

A front end or tool on its own address acts for a player once they allow it. It never handles their sign-in.

  1. Send the player to /auth/connect?site={origin}&return_to={url}&state={random} on the game's site. site is your origin (https://…, or http://localhost while developing); return_to is a page on it; state (up to 256 characters) comes back unchanged so you can match the answer to your request.
  2. The player signs in there if needed, sees your site's address and their house, and chooses Allow or Deny.
  3. They come back to return_to with the answer after the #: #token={token}&state={state}, or #error=denied&state={state}. Check state, then clear the address bar.
  4. Send the token with every request: Authorization: Bearer {token}. Any site may call the API this way (CORS is open; cookies are never used). For live updates, get a ticket from POST /live/ticket and open /live?ticket={ticket}.

A token lasts 30 days, until the player ends it, or until you call /auth/disconnect; then requests answer 401 and you connect again. It can do anything the player can do in the game, but it can't approve other sites, add passkeys or see the player's email.

Commands

Unit slots, wherever a command or reply lists units: 0 soldiers, 1 offense, 2 defense, 3 elite, 4 thieves, 5 offense+, 6 defense+, 7 elite+, 8 mercenaries, 9 elite++. A shorter list means 0 for the rest. In an attack, units[8] hires that many mercenaries for the attack (see Military). A train uses soldiers unless direct recruits straight from peasants. Spy reports show troops by kind instead: {"soldiers", "offense", "defense", "elites"}, base and upgraded together, thieves not shown.

Names are 1 to 40 characters for houses and states, and 1 to 30 for generals and academics. None may contain < or >, control characters or text-direction marks. Names come from players, so treat every one as plain text: escape it before you put it into HTML.

Leave out material in donate and grant to move gold. treasury orders, grant, set_tax, rename_state, set_research and the war commands, propose_work and vote_work are for the state leader only. propose_work puts forward your realm's work (your own state votes yes); any house in the realm may fund_work it once it has passed, with gold, the realm's material or both, each capped at what is still needed. A list without reserve and ticks takes offers instead of running an auction. Rates are in basis points: 300 means 3%.

An order fills at once against waiting orders (see Trade) and answers {"type": "order_placed", "order": 12, "filled": 100, "gold": 777, "fee": 36, "cross_fee": 21, "left": 20}: units filled at once, gold paid (a buy, fees included) or received (a sale, after the fee), the market fee, the cross-realm fee a buy paid (0 if every seller was in its realm), and units left waiting in the book. The fee rate is params.cross_realm_fee_bp in /rules; a buy holds room for it. News items order_filled carry fee and cross_fee for each fill, and treasury_share (materials: units by material; ticks: over how many) tells a house once a day what it got from its treasury's overflow. A cancel within {{order_lock_minutes}} minutes of placing is refused.

An operation or cast answers with chance: how good its chance was, as a band (very low, low, even, good, very good, or sure for a rite on yourself). The band edges are params.chance_bands_bp in /rules. The exact number isn't sent, because it would reveal the target's hidden thieves, adepts, Vigilance and Ward. A make_offer is only for a listed character.

Races and personalities are named by identity. units uses the slots above. medics, general, horses, chariots and kind (an id from Attacks; default assault) are optional in an attack. So are upgraded_mercenaries, how many of the hired mercenaries fight upgraded (races with mercenary upgrades only), and double_strike, to keep a second strike ready for second_strike (races with a double strike, at war, led by a general with enough traits). Give both realm and state to ask for a state, or neither (see placement).

Before the season starts, every command except create_house and POST /heirs/recall is refused: "the season hasn't started yet".

Staff

Staff run a live game through /dev/* requests (see Running the Next Age). GET /dev says whether you're staff and what you may do (can). The age requests:

RequestWhat it does
GET /dev/ageThis age, the planned next age with the game's check of it (plan_check), the age files to choose from (rulesets) and the lowest number the next age may take (admin)
POST /dev/age/planPlan the next age: {"ruleset", "number", "name", "start_at", "params", "note"}; checked by compiling, with every problem listed in errors (admin). DELETE drops the plan
POST /dev/age/endEnd the age now: {"note"} (owner)
POST /dev/age/openOpen the planned age once this one has ended: the world is archived and the server restarts into the new one (owner)

Answers

  • 200: done; the body says what happened (for example {"type": "created", "house": 0, "realm": 4, "state": 2, "seat": 1}).
  • 401: sign in first (see Signing In). Orders use your sign-in cookie, or a connected site's Authorization: Bearer token.
  • 422: the game refused the order; error says why (for example "that house isn't yours").
  • 429: too many requests. Orders allow a burst of 30, then 5 a second. Chat has its own limit: a burst of 5, then one message every 2 seconds (see Chat).

Reads may lag the game by up to a quarter of a second. What each request may show follows What You Can See: another house's hidden numbers show only in a report, from a successful intel operation (spy is the same as the dossier operation) or a divination.
