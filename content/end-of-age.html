An age lasts {{age_ticks}} ticks, about {{age_weeks}} weeks. On its last tick the age ends: every state is scored, one state wins, and each house keeps a few of its generals and academics as heirs for the next age.

Scoring

A state's score is the land of all its houses, plus {{score_per_war_point}} for every war point it won this age. The state with the highest score wins the age. Houses are also ranked by land and by renown.

Example. A state whose houses hold 12,000 acres and which won one war (2 points) scores 12,000 + 2 × {{score_per_war_point}}.

The standings are public all age long (GET /age, and the Age panel on your house page).

When it ends

  • Every house hears the winner, its state's rank, its own rank by land, and which heirs it keeps.
  • The world stops: no more commands, and ticks change nothing. The result stays public (GET /ages/{age}).
  • The next age starts with a new world and its own rules. Your account carries over.

Heirs

Before the age ends, mark the generals and academics you want to keep, in order of preference. You can change your choice any time until the last tick.

  • How many: heir slots come from your renown ({{heir_slot_list}}). Houses of the winning state get {{heir_winner_slots}} more at the end.
  • Which: the first ones you marked, as many as you have slots for, if they're still yours.
  • What travels: each heir leaves with a sealed chronicle of its whole service record and waits in your account, not in the world.

Marked heirs can't be courted.

Bringing an heir back

In a later age, once you have a house, recall an heir from the Age panel (or POST /heirs/recall). It joins your house with its record, starting a new stint, and arrives settling in like a traded character. You need room for it: fewer than the most generals a house may keep, or the universities for another academic. If the new age has no such trait or attribute any more, that one is dropped. Each heir comes back once.
