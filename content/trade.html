Every realm makes one material each tick. Its states share the output, each state splits its part between its houses and its treasury, and anyone can buy or sell on the market.

Shares

A realm's output is divided among its {{states_per_realm}} states by share. Shares start equal, about {{equal_share_pct}}% each, and never fall below {{share_floor_pct}}%. Winning an intra-realm war moves share from the loser to the winner.

Houses and treasury

Of each state's part, {{house_split_pct}}% is shared equally among its houses. The rest goes to the state's treasury, which only the state leader can use. The split is set per age.

Worked example. A bauxite realm makes {{bauxite_output}} units a tick. A state with a {{equal_share_pct}}% share gets about {{example_state_output}} a tick. {{house_split_pct}}% of that, {{example_houses_part}}, is shared among the state's houses, and {{example_treasury_part}} goes to the treasury. With {{houses_per_state}} houses, each gets about {{example_per_house}} a tick; leftovers carry over, so no unit is ever lost.

Seasons

Output isn't steady. Each material runs through seasons: over {{season_ticks}} ticks its output goes {{season_curve_pct}} of its base, and then round again. On average it makes its base output.

The materials are out of step: each runs some ticks ahead of the start ({{season_offsets}}). So while one material is scarce, another is plentiful. Buying in a glut and selling in a shortage pays, if you can store the goods.

The market page shows each material's output this tick and its season.

Spoilage

Goods don't keep forever. Each tick, a house may hold {{spoil_free}} units of each material, plus {{spoil_free_per_acre}} per acre of land, without loss. A treasury may hold {{spoil_treasury_free}} of each.

Beyond that, the excess spoils. You lose {{spoil_step_pct}}% of it for every free allowance's worth you're over, up to {{spoil_max_pct}}% a tick. A little over costs almost nothing; a big hoard melts fast.

Worked example. A house of 400 acres may keep 450 of each material. With 900 bauxite it's 450 over, one allowance's worth, so it loses 1% of 450: 4 units. With 4,950 it's 4,500 over, ten allowances' worth, so the {{spoil_max_pct}}% cap applies: 225 units a tick.

Goods in your sell orders count, so an order at a price nobody pays won't protect them. They spoil after your stock, starting with your highest-priced order. Your house page shows what would spoil next tick.

Depletion

Some ages switch depletion on (this age: {{depletion}}). Then each realm's deposit holds {{depletion_reserve_ticks}} ticks of its base output. As the deposit is worked, output falls with the square of the share used up: hardly at first, steeply near the end. It never falls below {{depletion_floor_pct}}% of the base. Materials are plentiful early in such an age and scarce late.

The market

  • Each material has one order book. You post a buy (the most you'll pay per unit) or a sell (the least you'll take).
  • Orders fill as soon as they're placed. A new order trades at once with waiting orders that meet its price: the best price first, then the oldest. Each trade is at the waiting order's price, so a buy at 8 that meets a sell at 6 pays 6.
  • Whatever doesn't fill at once waits in the book at your price, until someone's order meets it or you cancel it.
  • Placing an order sets its goods or gold aside at once. A buy sets aside its price for every unit plus room for the fee; what it doesn't spend comes back straight away.
  • The book shows prices and quantities, never who placed them.

The market fee

The fee is paid by the order that fills against a waiting order: {{market_fee_pct}}% of the gold that trade is worth. A buyer pays it on top of the price; a seller has it taken from the gold received. The order that was waiting pays nothing. So posting an order and waiting is free, and taking what's already in the book costs the fee. Treasury orders pay it too.

The fee is destroyed; it goes to no one. (The {{trade_fee_pct}}% fee to the seller's state is a separate one, for selling characters in the Hall of Deeds.)

Trading houses cut your fee, down to nothing with enough of them. The fee never goes below zero, so trading back and forth with a friend can only lose gold.

Worked example. The book has sells of 40 bauxite at 6 and 60 at 8. You place a buy of 120 at 8. At once you get 40 at 6 (240 gold) and 60 at 8 (480 gold): 720 gold, plus a {{market_fee_pct}}% fee on it if you have no trading houses. The other 20 wait in the book at 8. If a seller later fills them, you pay 160 gold and no fee. Your news shows each fill and its fee.

Cancelling

An order stands for {{order_lock_minutes}} minutes after it's placed; only then can you cancel it. This keeps the book honest: a price you post is one others can actually trade against. Cancelling gives back the goods or gold the order still holds.

Trading with yourself

You can buy and sell the same material at once, as a market maker does: for example, buy bauxite at 9 and sell it at 11. You just can't trade with yourself, so your buy must be priced below your own sell. A buy at or above it is refused, because it would fill against your own sell. For a state's leader, their own house and the treasury count as one trader.

States that make the same material may agree on prices between themselves. The game doesn't enforce such agreements; keeping them is up to the players.

What materials are for

  • {{upgrade_material}}: upgrading units.
  • {{medic_material}}: medics.
  • {{general_material}}: generals.
  • pine: refined into timber (chariots) or resin (incense for hexes and vigils, and the Torch attack).
  • limestone: forts, castles, watchstones and the Ancestral Hall (see Buildings).
  • paper: extra books in science, and academics.
  • sulfur also pays for the Set Fires operation, and amber for the Ancestral Hall.

Giving and granting

Any house can give gold or materials to its state's treasury. The leader can grant treasury gold or materials to any house in the state, and the house's news says so.
