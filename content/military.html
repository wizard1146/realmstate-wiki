Every race has an offense specialist, a defense specialist, an elite and a thief, plus upgraded offense+, defense+ and elite+ units. Each race's units and their numbers are on its race page.

Training

  • Each unit costs gold (its price, times your training cost modifier) and one peasant.
  • Troops are ready exactly {{train_ticks}} hours later. While training they still eat.
  • Up to 1,000,000 at a time.

Upgrades

You can upgrade offense specialists, defense specialists and elites you own into their + form. An upgraded unit's main stat is {{upgrade_bonus_pct}}% higher (offense+ in offense, defense+ in defense, elite+ in both), unless its race sets its own numbers.

  • Cost: {{upgrade_cost}} {{upgrade_material}} per unit, {{upgrade_cost_elite}} per elite. See Materials.
  • The units are in training for {{train_ticks}} hours, then return upgraded.

Medics

Medics don't fight; they save troops who would die in battle. A medic costs {{medic_gold}} gold, {{medic_material_cost}} {{medic_material}} and a peasant, and is ready after {{train_ticks}} hours.

  • Each medic per 100 troops in a fight saves {{rescue_per_medic_pct}}% of that side's deaths, up to {{rescue_cap_pct}}%.
  • Medics you send with an attack protect your attackers and come home with the army. Medics at home protect your defenders.
  • Example: 400 troops sent with 40 medics is 10 medics per 100 troops, so 20% of the deaths are saved.

Horses and chariots

  • Stables breed horses each tick, up to what they can hold.
  • A horse adds {{horse_offense}} offense to the troop it carries; a chariot adds {{chariot_offense}}. One mount per troop sent (thieves don't ride).
  • A chariot is built from {{chariot_horses}} horse, {{chariot_material_cost}} {{chariot_material}} and {{chariot_gold}} gold. Timber is refined from pine.
  • Mounts die at the same rate as your attackers; medics don't save them.

Attacking

You choose how many troops of each unit slot to send. The battle resolves at once.

  1. Offense = the offense of every unit sent (elites times your elite offense modifier), times your offense modifier, times battle luck (up to ±{{luck_pct}}%). A general leading the army adds its traits.
  2. Defense = the defense of every unit the target has at home (elites times their elite defense modifier), times their defense modifier, plus 1 point per acre they own. Their defending general adds its traits.
  3. If offense is higher, you win and take {{land_gain_pct}}% of their land. They lose it at once; you receive it when your army returns. You take more from a state that is Unfriendly or Hostile toward yours, and more still from one you're at war with.
  4. Win or lose, {{attacker_loss_pct}}% of the troops you sent die and {{defender_loss_pct}}% of the target's troops at home die, less what each side's medics save.
  5. A win earns renown.
  6. Your army comes home {{attack_return_ticks}} hours later, times your return time modifier.

Troops that are away don't defend your house.

You can't attack houses in your own state, or across a ceasefire. Every attack raises the target state's hostility meter toward yours.

Renown

Renown is public, like land. A successful attack earns up to {{renown_per_win}} renown, scaled by how close the fight was: defense ÷ offense. Beating a well-defended house earns the most; crushing a defenceless one earns little. Attacks on a state you are at war with earn {{war_renown_pct}}% more, and winning a war gives every house in the state {{war_win_renown}}. Renown unlocks better generals.

What you learn

You see your own offense, whether you won, the land taken and the losses on both sides, but never the defender's defense. The defender's news tells them who attacked and what they lost. To size up a target first, spy on it.

Thieves, the fourth unit slot, don't fight. They are used for spying.
