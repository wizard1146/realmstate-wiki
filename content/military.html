Every house has soldiers, drafted from its peasants. Every race also has an offense specialist, a defense specialist, an elite and a thief, plus upgraded offense+, defense+ and elite+ units. Unit slots run in that order: soldiers, offense, defense, elite, thief, then the three upgrades. Each race's units and their numbers are on its race page.

Soldiers and the draft

{{soldier_name}} fight at {{soldier_off}} offense and {{soldier_def}} defense, the same for every race, and cost nothing: they're drafted from your peasants.

  • Your draft rate is the share of your population you want under arms: troops at home, away and in training, soldiers included. New houses start at {{draft_default_pct}}%; you can set 0 to {{draft_max_pct}}%.
  • Each tick, while you're below your rate, up to {{draft_speed_pct}}% of your peasants are drafted, never past the rate. Set 0 to stop drafting.
  • Drafted peasants no longer pay gold, so a high rate costs income.

Training

Soldiers train into specialists, elites and thieves.

  • Each costs gold (its price, times your training cost modifier) and one soldier.
  • Timing is a bell. Most trainees are ready after {{train_ticks}} ticks. The rest finish spread up to {{train_spread_ticks}} ticks earlier or later; the fewer, the further from the middle. Trainees still eat.
  • Not everyone makes it. {{train_fail_pct}}% fail and go back to being soldiers; their gold is lost. {{train_death_pct}}% die in training.
  • Some excel. {{train_plus_pct}}% of trainees into offense, defense or elite units come out as the upgraded (+) unit at once, at no extra cost.
  • A general from the ranks. Training elites has a very small chance ({{train_general_per_1000_pct}}% per 1,000 elites) of raising a general for free, if you have room for one. No elites are retired for it.
  • Up to 1,000,000 at a time.

Recruiting straight from peasants

You can skip the draft and recruit specialists, elites and thieves straight from your peasants, at a price:

  • {{direct_cost_pct}}% more gold, and {{direct_time_pct}}% longer to train.
  • A wider bell: up to {{direct_spread_ticks}} ticks either side of the average.
  • {{direct_fail_pct}}% fail (back to peasants, the gold lost) and {{direct_death_pct}}% die.
  • None come out upgraded.

Upgrades

You can upgrade offense specialists, defense specialists and elites you own into their + form. An upgraded unit's main stat is {{upgrade_bonus_pct}}% higher (offense+ in offense, defense+ in defense, elite+ in both), unless its race sets its own numbers. Offense+ also gains {{upgrade_cross_point}} defense, and defense+ {{upgrade_cross_point}} offense.

  • Cost: {{upgrade_cost}} {{upgrade_material}} per unit, {{upgrade_cost_elite}} per elite. See Materials.
  • The units are in training for {{train_ticks}} hours, then return upgraded.

Medics

Medics don't fight; they save troops who would die in battle. A medic costs {{medic_gold}} gold, {{medic_material_cost}} {{medic_material}} and a peasant, and is ready after {{train_ticks}} hours.

  • Each medic per 100 troops in a fight saves {{rescue_per_medic_pct}}% of that side's deaths, up to {{rescue_cap_pct}}%.
  • Medics you send with an attack protect your attackers and come home with the army. Medics at home protect your defenders.
  • Example: 400 troops sent with 40 medics is 10 medics per 100 troops, so 20% of the deaths are saved.

Horses and chariots

  • Stables breed horses each tick, up to what they can hold.
  • A horse adds {{horse_offense}} offense to the troop it carries; a chariot adds {{chariot_offense}}. One mount per troop sent (thieves don't ride).
  • A chariot is built from {{chariot_horses}} horse, {{chariot_material_cost}} {{chariot_material}} and {{chariot_gold}} gold. Timber is refined from pine.
  • Mounts die at the same rate as your attackers; medics don't save them.

Attacking

You choose how many troops of each unit slot to send, and the kind of attack: an Assault for land (the default), or Breach, Reclaim, Torch, Sack, Massacre or Blockade; within your own state, only Raze, for clearing out idle houses. Every kind fights the same battle; they differ in what a win takes and what they need. The battle resolves at once.

  1. Offense = the offense of every unit sent (elites times your elite offense modifier), times your offense modifier, times battle luck (up to ±{{luck_pct}}%). A general leading the army adds its traits.
  2. Defense = the defense of every unit the target has at home (elites times their elite defense modifier), times their defense modifier, plus 1 point per acre they own. Their defending general adds its traits.
  3. If offense is higher, you win. An assault takes {{land_gain_pct}}% of their land; other kinds take what the Attacks table says. They lose it at once; you receive it when your army returns. You take more from a state that is Unfriendly or Hostile toward yours, and more still from one you're at war with.
  4. Win or lose, {{attacker_loss_pct}}% of the troops you sent die and {{defender_loss_pct}}% of the target's troops at home die, less what each side's medics save.
  5. An assault, breach or reclaim that wins earns renown.
  6. Sacked goods travel home with the army and arrive when it does.
  7. A house hit often lately loses less to each new hit (see protection from repeated hits).
  8. Your army comes home {{attack_return_ticks}} hours later, times your return time modifier.

Troops that are away don't defend your house.

You can't attack houses in your own state (except to raze an idle one), across a ceasefire, or while either house is under protection. Every attack raises the target state's hostility meter toward yours.

Renown

Renown is public, like land. A successful attack earns up to {{renown_per_win}} renown, scaled by how close the fight was: defense ÷ offense. Beating a well-defended house earns the most; crushing a defenceless one earns little. Attacks on a state you are at war with earn {{war_renown_pct}}% more.

Renown is never spent. Where it comes from:

  • Attacks: a winning assault, breach or reclaim (above). Other kinds of attack earn none.
  • Wars: winning a war gives every house in the state {{war_win_renown}}; a peace gives both sides {{war_peace_renown}}; the loser gets none.
  • Colloquium: {{renown_per_thousand_books}} for every 1,000 books you give to a project.
  • Ancestral Hall: renown every tick.

The renown gain modifier (from race, personality and the Heraldry project) raises renown from attacks, the Colloquium and the Ancestral Hall, but not from wars.

What it does:

  • More trait slots for your generals (at {{general_trait_renown}} renown), and the right to choose traits instead of drawing them at {{general_pick_renown}}.
  • Heir slots at the end of the age ({{heir_slot_list}}).
  • Rankings: houses are ranked by renown at the end of the age. It doesn't count toward the state's score.

What you learn

You see your own offense, whether you won, the land taken and the losses on both sides, but never the defender's defense. The defender's news tells them who attacked and what they lost. To size up a target first, spy on it.

Thieves, the fourth unit slot, don't fight. They are used for spying.
